# Introduction–Results Citation Separation Contract — v3.0

## Purpose
The manuscript must distinguish citations used to establish the scholarly background from citations used as evidence for Results and Discussion. A paper cited in the Introduction does not need to be cited again in Results or Discussion merely because it is part of the same literature set.

## Citation roles
Assign citations internally to one or more roles:
- `background`: establishes field context, established knowledge, prior approaches, historical development, or terminology;
- `gap`: supports limitations, inconsistencies, unresolved problems, or the rationale for the review;
- `finding`: supports an empirical or quantitative finding reported by an included study;
- `synthesis`: supports a cross-study pattern or thematic interpretation;
- `method`: supports a methodological statement when genuinely needed.

A single record may have multiple roles only when the manuscript sections make genuinely different evidence claims from that record.

## Introduction citation policy
The Introduction should preferentially use `background` and `gap` citations. It may cite included studies when their abstracts provide suitable field-level evidence, but the Introduction should not become a preview of the Results.

Where possible, use background/gap citations that are not subsequently needed as finding-level evidence. This creates a clear scholarly distinction between why the topic matters and what the review found.

The Introduction must not repeat a Results finding merely to populate the background. If a numerical result is essential to establish the research problem, it may be mentioned once in the Introduction with its citation; otherwise reserve numerical study findings for Results and Discussion.

## Results citation policy
Results citations must support actual findings, quantitative results, study characteristics, or evidence patterns reported by included records. Do not cite a paper in Results solely because it appeared in the Introduction.

If a record was cited in the Introduction only for background/gap purposes and does not contribute a Results claim, do not repeat it in Results.

When a study contributes a quantitative finding, the Results citation must be attached to the finding and may repeat an Introduction citation only when the same study genuinely supports both roles.

## Discussion citation policy
The Discussion section MUST NOT contain any citations. Any interpretation of the results, contradictions, implications, or evidence gaps must be discussed without adding or repeating in-text citations.

The Discussion must remain purely an interpretation of already cited Results and must not introduce new literature-dependent claims that require citations.

## Reference-list consequence
The reference list must contain every source cited anywhere in the manuscript, regardless of role. A background source that appears only in the Introduction remains in the reference list. Do not remove it simply because it is absent from Results and Discussion.

## Validation
Before finalization:
- every citation has a role and evidence basis;
- Introduction citations are not duplicated in Results/Discussion without a substantive reason;
- Results citations support actual findings rather than background context;
- the Discussion section MUST NOT contain any citations;
- references cited only in the Introduction remain in the final reference list;
- NO citation is used more than 3 times throughout the entire manuscript;
- the manuscript must list more than 100 references.
